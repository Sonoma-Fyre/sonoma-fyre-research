# Full interview record log

Team 12: Reversing Declining Wine Demand, then Building Sonoma's Small Business Innovation Ecosystem (Sonoma Proof).

Compiled by Allison Leow outside the repo and committed here 2026-08-14 so the record is version-controlled and greppable alongside the note files. The source is `interview-log.docx.pdf`, held locally in `team-model-proposal/` and not committed, consistent with how other raw captures are handled.

All field interviews are split into two phases: the original idea (Fairmont Events, reversing declining wine demand through winery-led experiences) run through 2026-08-08, and the eventual idea (Sonoma Proof, a growth lab for Sonoma small businesses) which the team pivoted to starting 2026-08-11. Interviews are grouped by which phase was live on the date they were conducted.

Each conversation is read through two lenses regardless of phase: what it said about declining wine demand and the need for experiences that attract younger buyers, and what concrete innovations or tactics the interviewee described trying.

## Phase 1: original idea, before 2026-08-11 (Fairmont Events, declining wine demand)

### Chuck (last name not recorded), Wine reseller, The Wine Mine, Oakland
- Date: 2026-07-24. Conducted by Allison Leow and Alexa Kornau.
- Connection: retail buyer, a gatekeeper between small Sonoma wineries and shelf space.
- Declining demand: retail ticket sizes have shrunk from cases to single bottles or totes over two to four years. Calls the industry "getting ready to panic," comparing the mood to 2008-2010. Tariffs and rising MSRPs squeeze price-sensitive buyers. Wine club has held around six to eight bottles, capped near $100, because a reliable $15-20 bottle is hard to find. Names hard seltzers, kombucha, and premixed cocktails as competitors for wine's occasions.
- Innovations tried: diversified well beyond wine, expanding sake, adding gluten-free beer, keeping NA wine and cocktails in stock year-round rather than seasonally, adding sparkling tea and tinned-fish-and-chocolate pairings. Shifted from type-based shelf talk to curated tasting-based themes. Testing Uber Eats delivery. Names lightweight glass and tetra, can, and bag-in-box packaging as the industry's last big innovation wave, driven partly by carbon-footprint concerns.

### Justine (last name not recorded), Operations manager, OneHope
- Date: 2026-07-27. Conducted by Alexa Kornau.
- Connection: winery-side operations and channel mix.
- Declining demand: demand has dropped since 2023 through post-COVID normalization, Gen Z not drinking, and inflation. DTC is flat while the winery-visit channel's average spend has risen a little. Notes a broader health kick that started during COVID.
- Innovations tried: cans were "a hit for a while" as a pandemic-era small-format innovation. Actively exploring NA wine as a new product line.

### Name not recorded, Tasting-room and DTC manager, Highway 12
- Date: 2026-07-29. Conducted by Alexa Kornau.
- Connection: tasting-room and DTC operations at a mid-size Sonoma winery.
- Declining demand: average customer is 50-plus, and he sees and hears that younger people are drinking less. Suggests the industry's own rising tasting fees may be self-inflicted. Some higher-priced wineries in his network closed in the past year.
- Innovations tried: built a new communal club letting members pick across the whole portfolio instead of a winery-picked selection, directly lowering the barrier to join, called "exceptional in creating new membership." Moved phone and email marketing in-house, cheaper than the outside agency previously used, and grew sales through active telesales. Runs monthly phone specials and futures offers that reliably generate referrals.

### Julian Christiansen, Winemaker, Greater Fool Wine
- Date: 2026-08-04. Self-completed form, full five-person team.
- Connection: small winery, self-reported operator data point.
- Declining demand: club membership is up but the renewal share is down, a churn signal under a headline that looks fine. Customers cite price and value, quality, and brand loyalty. Thin response overall; treat as directional.
- Innovations tried: uses lower-cost Austrian oak instead of French oak on its Sauvignon Blanc, a cost-conscious quality move. Co-ferments red varietals like Charbono and Petite Sirah, an old-technique revival meant to give each wine its own identity. DTC share of revenue is rising.

### Valerie Wilson, Tasting room manager, Sebastiani Winery
- Date: 2026-08-03. Conducted by Rohit Mishra.
- Connection: established, larger Sonoma winery; contrast case to smaller and newer producers.
- Declining demand: decline attributed primarily to the post-COVID economy. Discretionary spending has dropped and wine, especially Sonoma wine, is often the first cut. A $120 to $150 tasting fee for under 40 minutes is a real barrier to bringing in newer, younger visitors.
- Innovations tried: cut club allocation from 12 bottles to four or five per shipment and adjusted the discount structure. Increased social media investment. Introduced a mixed red-and-white pack so members do not choose bottle by bottle. Added unlimited complimentary tastings for club members year-round, with one guest included.
- See `2026-08-03-sebastiani-valerie-wilson-notes.md`.

### Victoria (last name not recorded), Wine educator, runs the downtown tasting room, Sosie Wines
- Date: 2026-08-03. Conducted by Rohit Mishra.
- Connection: small, newer Sonoma producer of about 1,500 cases; direct contrast to Sebastiani's scale.
- Declining demand: attributes the slowdown mainly to the economy and political uncertainty. Winery is three years old, a strong first year then steady decline. Sunday foot traffic dropped from three to five tastings to typically one. Rising tasting prices, needed to manage cost, may be compounding the drop.
- Innovations tried: investing in Google marketing and social media. Built strong personal engagement as the face of the brand; reviews frequently name her directly. Its GSM blend, one of few offered locally, is called out by customer reviews as a standout differentiator.
- See `2026-08-03-sosie-victoria-notes.md`.

### Mary (last name not recorded), Director of sales finance, western area, Gallo
- Date: 2026-08-05. Conducted by Alexa Kornau.
- Connection: large-scale producer view across a luxury and mass portfolio, contrast case to boutique Sonoma wineries.
- Declining demand: broad decline since the post-COVID peak across wine and beer. Luxury tasting-room visits down "pretty dramatically." Wine clubs skew boomer, with GLP-1 and inflation named as pressures. Frames wine's barrier for younger buyers bluntly, as feeling "hoity-toity" next to a screw-top rosé served over ice.
- Innovations tried: NFL and celebrity sponsorships (Barefoot) to embed wine in culture the way beer and soda are. VMC, a Mexican-inspired flavor brand aimed at an underserved Hispanic market. Tetra packs and "beat boxes," a Gen-Z-oriented wine cooler, plus canned wine, though taste and portion size were a problem. Rambauer's low-cost tactic of inviting club members instead of entertaining buyers, which built unusually strong community. Acquiring Gen-Z-founded brands (Winey Baby) for approachable packaging rather than building one in-house.

### Jay Boberg, Co-founder, Nicolas-Jay Winery
- Date: 2026-08-08. Conducted by Allison Leow.
- Connection: owner-level view spanning two wine regions, DTC economics.
- Declining demand: names GLP-1 drugs as likely the single biggest driver of decline, though skeptical Gen Z's decline is structural, citing a UK survey showing steady 18-to-23 consumption. Napa's tasting fees ($75-125) and hotel rates ($500-600 a night) are a real backlash driver, with Napa hotels already cutting rates 15-20%. Runs a five-times-a-year chef dinner series (Chez Panisse, Gramercy Tavern, a top Tokyo sushi chef) that draws a food-first, notably younger crowd, 30-year-olds "spending $300 per dinner without hesitation," direct evidence of what pulls younger buyers toward wine-adjacent experiences.
- Innovations tried: moved the wine club to flexible mix-and-match allocations to cut resistance to committing. Distributes a quarterly harvest playlist by QR code to club members first, then in the tasting room. Names Cho Winery (Oregon) as best-in-class on social media for a dog-friendly, family-forward brand.

### Jo (last name not recorded), Tasting room manager, Meadowcroft Wines
- Date: 2026-08-08. Conducted by Alexa Kornau.
- Connection: small boutique wine producer in Sonoma.
- Declining demand: the property has been through ownership turnover and bankruptcy. Bought by Mattson along with many other Sonoma properties, it went bankrupt and was sold about eight months ago to the Singh family, who also own the sugar mill, with many former tenants moving into downtown Sonoma. On-site for 16 years, Jo describes a generational drop-off directly: baby boomer visitors have stopped coming and a new generation never picked up in their place. Attributes some of Gen Z's disinterest to greater health consciousness. Pricing here also runs higher than downtown Sonoma, a barrier relative to nearby competition.
- Innovations tried: runs an unusually accessible wine club with complimentary tastings, a minimum of just four bottles per shipment, flat $15 shipping at six bottles or more, and flexible participation that lets members skip two, three, or four shipments rather than commit continuously, explicitly designed to keep people engaged without penalizing those who cannot commit regularly. Held a ribbon-cutting on April 24 meant to build momentum for the property, but it did not translate into sustained traction; only a couple of shops are currently open, though the cafe runs seven days a week and a food truck operates Thursday through Saturday. Has hosted themed community events to draw different audiences.

## Phase 2: eventual idea, 2026-08-11 onward (Sonoma Proof, growth lab for small businesses)

### Prema Kerollis, Co-founder and president, Three Sticks Winery
- Date: 2026-08-12. Conducted by Allison Leow, with Rohit Mishra, Alexa Kornau, Rodolfo Lagos, and Jiahan Ericsson also present.
- Connection: winery founder reacting to the team's early event concept; stress-tested the idea before the pivot fully landed.
- Declining demand: visitors are spending less on bottles but more on experiences, including at competing venues like the restaurant Enclos. People are drinking wine on fewer occasions and collecting less. Argued wineries need to speak more broadly about wine itself, not pitch one bottle, to reach a new consumer. Younger drinkers want clear value and easy entry points. Family picnics at wineries, a way younger families used to engage, have largely fallen away.
- Growth-lab relevant: skeptical the team's original big-event concept would work, calling it high-effort and too similar to BottleRock to be transformative, an early signal that pushed the team toward a broader model. Sonoma's 19 AVA alliances are merging into one more aligned voice, a coordination model relevant to a cross-business cohort. Floated dealcoholized wine, expensive to produce, and a half-bottle format as responses to more single-person households.
- See `2026-08-12-three-sticks-prema-kerollis-notes.md`.

### Matthew Wood, COO, Hess Persson
- Date: 2026-08-12. Conducted by Rohit Mishra, Alexa Kornau, Rodolfo Lagos, Allison Leow, and Jiahan Ericsson.
- Connection: winery leadership view on generational demand and tourism infrastructure, informing the shift toward a broader small-business framing.
- Declining demand: high-frequency drinkers consume 80% of all wine, and the generations behind them (Gen Alpha) are smaller, too small to replace millennials as they age out. Day trippers are 62% of tasting-room volume, a more transient, harder-to-retain visitor than an overnight guest. Compared the downturn to a 1980s oversupply cycle. Gen Z and millennial women spend three times more of their budget on discretionary items than the comparison group discussed, competing directly with wine for that spend.
- Growth-lab relevant: no specific tried innovation described. Raised tourism-infrastructure questions, building more hotels and changing Airbnb restrictions, as a potential lever rather than something already tried.
- See `2026-08-12-hess-persson-matthew-wood-notes.md`.

### Martìn Gobbèe, General manager, Smash Burger
- Date: 2026-08-12. Conducted by Rohit Mishra, Alexa Kornau, Rodolfo Lagos, Allison Leow, and Jiahan Ericsson.
- Connection: first non-wine Sonoma small business interviewed; directly supports the pivot to a small-business-wide growth lab rather than a wine-only solution.
- Declining demand: not a wine business and did not speak to wine demand directly. His account of Sonoma's slow, early-closing business environment, unlike New York City, helps explain why the county is a difficult place to build any new visitor-facing experience, wine included.
- Growth-lab relevant: describes Smash Burger's own experiments to diversify traffic and revenue despite a difficult local permitting and ADA-compliance environment: hosting weddings and other private events upstairs, and hosting World Cup viewing parties, both outside the restaurant's core business, exactly the kind of cross-category tactic the growth lab wants to surface and share.
- See `2026-08-12-smash-burger-martin-gobbee-notes.md`.

### Tim Zahner, Executive Director, Sonoma Valley Visitors Bureau
- Date: 2026-08-12. Conducted by Allison Leow and Jiahan Ericsson.
- Connection: directly shaped the Sonoma Proof cohort model.
- Declining demand: not about wine demand directly. His point was that wineries cannot easily collaborate with each other because they are in direct competition, which cuts against a wine-only version of any collective solution.
- Growth-lab relevant: argued a cross-industry cohort, businesses learning from people outside their own category rather than only from direct competitors, would work better than a wine-only group. This conversation is the direct seed of Sonoma Proof's cross-industry cohort design.
- See `2026-08-12-tim-zahner-notes.md`.

### Mark Bodenhamer, CEO, Sonoma Valley Chamber of Commerce
- Date: 2026-08-12. Conducted by Rohit Mishra and Alexa Kornau.
- Connection: chamber-level view of the Sonoma small-business ecosystem, a natural partner or anchor for Sonoma Proof.
- Declining demand: not about wine demand directly.
- Growth-lab relevant: identified community and resident resistance, not the city, the Chamber, or local businesses, as the main force blocking growth and development in Sonoma (new hotels being one example), a friction that complicates plans like Bill Foley's development efforts. Argued any growth strategy, including a cohort growth lab, has to account for community buy-in and local political and social resistance, not just economics or operations.
