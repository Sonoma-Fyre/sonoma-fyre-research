#### Warm-up (anchor in his own role)

"Tell me about your role at Wine Mine, how long you've been there, and what a typical day looks like." Let him talk before anything structured. No capture line, this is rapport and context.

#### Core: price tiers and what's moving (E2)

"Which price tiers and wine styles are you still taking on right now, and what have you delisted in the last year, at what price points?"

Why we ask: locates where retail demand actually sits. Desk research calls this the hourglass, under-$12 deteriorating fastest while $20 to $29 and $100-plus hold up (svb-2026-state-of-us-wine-industry.pdf). Wine Mine's own positioning skews toward the segment desk data says is weakest, so this is a real test, not a formality. Capture: tiers and styles still moving, items delisted and their price points. Do not lead, this one is open by design in the guide. Ask, then wait.

#### Core: the gatekeeper question (E4)

"For a small Sonoma winery, who actually decides whether its wine gets on your shelf? Walk me through how a new winery gets in front of you and what makes you say yes or no."

Why we ask: names the real gatekeeping process between a Sonoma winery and its retail buyer, direct input to the ecosystem map. Capture: the actual decision process, who's involved, what makes a wine get stocked or dropped.

#### Core: regional marketing, if it fits (E3)

"Have you seen any shared or regional marketing effort, Sonoma or otherwise, actually move visits or sales at your shop? If so, what were the numbers?"

Why we ask: tests whether the parked Wine Improvement District idea (or any similar effort) has any retail-level evidence behind it, versus just being proposed. Capture: named efforts, measured change if any, hard figures if he has them.
