## Wine Demand Decline: Systemic Drivers

Media cycle overstating the trend, but repetition creates its own momentum

GLP-1 drugs (Ozempic etc.) likely the single biggest direct cause of reduced consumption

20-34M Americans have taken them, mostly 40+, historically heavy wine drinkers

White wine decline especially visible

People coming off the drugs still drinking very little: habit broken after 6-9 months

Cannabis gummies also a factor, both as a substitute and because they amplify alcohol’s effect

Skeptical that younger generations are inherently drinking less: no solid data, mostly anecdotal

UK survey showed 18-23 year olds drinking as much as prior generations, just different categories

Fine wine ($50+) audience was always primarily 40-70, so Gen Z decline is largely irrelevant to the segment

Interested in what the research team brings out of it, definitely some smoke, and these systemic things are real

## Tourism and Tasting Room Traffic

Willamette Valley foot traffic down vs. 2022-2024; 2025-2026 flat but below prior peak

Theory: post-COVID pent-up demand has shifted back to Europe travel

Napa backlash more acute: tasting fees $75-125, hotels $500-600/night, cabs $175-400+

Napa hotels reportedly cut rates 15-20% in response

45% of Nickel and Nickel revenue comes from tasting room and wine club combined

15% drop in foot traffic has direct downstream impact on club signups and bottle sales

## Direct-to-Consumer Strategy and Revenue Mix

Wine club is the highest-margin channel, near-certain revenue (~$700-800K per six-month shipment)

Without it, the business would not survive

Moved to flexible allocations (mix-and-match) to reduce consumer resistance to commitment

Single-vineyard Pinots sold exclusively at the winery, creating a pull incentive to join the club

One SKU sold only through retail/restaurants, deliberately excluded from club and winery

Revenue mix: 45% tasting room and club, 22% export, ~38% restaurants and retail

Export currently hit hard: Canada and China/Hong Kong markets lost (~15% of export) due to politics

COVID showed the resilience of the club channel when restaurant revenue collapsed

Restaurant economics are structurally unfavorable for wineries

Winery sells at $25, distributor sells at $45, restaurant lists at $120-160

Winery margin: ~$5-8; restaurant margin: $65-115 on the same bottle

Three-tier distribution (Alexander Hamilton-era law) prevents direct restaurant sales outside Oregon

Cult wineries (e.g. Williams Selyem) that went fully direct faced concentration risk

Lost ~50% of mailing list revenue in 2007-2008 recession, forced to add restaurant channel

## Audience Building and Community

Music-to-wine parallel: both are emotional, identity-driven purchases, not purely analytical

Pulling out a bottle of Nickel and Nickel is like wearing a band t-shirt, signals who you are

Chef dinner series: 5 per year, featuring Chez Panisse, Gramercy Tavern, top Tokyo sushi chef

Draws food-first crowd who then enter the wine community; 30-year-olds spending $300/dinner without hesitation

Quarterly harvest playlist: one-third artist-managed catalog, one-third established music, one-third new discoveries

Distributed via QR code to wine club members first, then played in tasting room and shared online

June wine club event: nearly half of attendees were under 50, considered a meaningful win

Cho winery (Oregon) cited as best-in-class social media operator: dog-friendly, baby-changing station, family identity built into brand
