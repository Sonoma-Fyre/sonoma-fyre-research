Experiences

Alone you have one audience. Together you have ten.

The cohort invents its own shared events during the lab, then runs them together over the quarter. Every business feeds customers to the next, and each new cohort adds to a growing network, so the draw compounds.

Each cohort designs and runs two shared events over the quarter. The cohort decides what they are. Nothing is handed down, and no two cohorts have to land in the same place.

Two examples, so the outcome is concrete. Treat them as the shape of the thing, not the menu.

Example

The Sonoma Passport

A discount trail across the cohort. Customers hop from winery to creamery to kitchen, collecting stamps and deals in a printed passport, and every stop feeds the next.

Example

Culinary Day

One shared food-and-drink day. Each business puts on something special, and a single route walks guests through all of them.

What owners test

An experiment is an event guests can walk into.

The innovation you prototype in the lab becomes something real customers try. A few of the shapes it takes:

Winery

Tap vs glass, blind

A blind panel of regulars rates a new pour and votes on what earns a spot on the menu.

Creamery

An ice-cream workshop

Turn a slow afternoon into a paid, hands-on class that people book out.

Maker

Crochet and wine

Pair a craft night with a local pour and test a ticketed, repeatable format.

Kitchen

Sip and smash

A burger-and-wine pop-up with a cohort winery, built to fill a dead night.

Why it compounds

The network is the moat.

Cohort one builds a trail of eight to ten businesses. Cohort two adds more. Within a year the trail is a destination in its own right, and each new cohort joins a collective that already has an audience. That is the thing no generic accelerator can copy.
